DAVIS

A Depth-Only End-to-End Active-Vision Framework for Humanoid Soccer Skills

One closed-loop policy maps head-mounted depth, proprioceptive history, and optional task commands directly to 25-DoF joint targets for dynamic soccer contact.

Paper Rebuttal Method Results

Abstract

Humanoid soccer contact skills require perception, approach, alignment, impact, and recovery while the robot's own motion changes its viewpoint. DAVIS asks whether a humanoid can learn these skills using only a head-mounted depth image, proprioceptive history, and an optional low-dimensional command—without runtime perception or planning modules. Training-time visibility-aware auxiliary geometry, GT-to-prediction annealing, task curricula, and AMP-style motion priors bridge privileged simulation supervision and real deployment. We instantiate the framework on goal-directed shooting and directional dribbling, and validate it in IsaacLab and on a Noetix E1 humanoid.

Method

Structure lives in the learning signal—not in a brittle deployment stack. Auxiliary heads predict task geometry and visibility from the depth latent; confidence gating and annealing gradually move the actor from privileged guidance to self-prediction.

01 · Encode

Compact visual latent

A lightweight depth encoder turns the single depth frame into a 32-D representation. A history encoder adds body-state context.

02 · Ground

Geometry × visibility

Ball and goal geometry are gated by learned visibility confidence, so uncertain estimates do not dominate whole-body control.

03 · Anneal

Privileged → deployable

GT-to-prediction annealing teaches how geometry should affect motion before the policy operates entirely on depth-derived features.

Skill instantiations

Goal-directed shooting

Approach, align, and strike toward a goal under varied initializations. The policy receives no operator command and closes the loop through depth and proprioception.

Directional dribbling

Maintain repeated foot–ball contacts while following a low-dimensional direction command, including target reaching, obstacle traversal, and slalom dribbling.

Results

Robot trials

Rebuttal trials report shooting successes of 26/38, 28/49, and 33/60 for Easy/Medium/Hard conditions; dribbling reaches 22/28, 19/28, and 17/28 across three settings.

Failures are dominated by object loss from the depth view and overly forceful contacts. The active-vision policy reorients to reacquire the ball.
